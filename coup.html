House rules

Coup: new rules

Coup with the Reformation expansion, with one twist: the Loyalist and Reformist cards are hidden, so nobody knows who is on their side.

Setup

  1. Play with the Reformation expansion: Inquisitor in the deck and a Treasury in the middle.
  2. Faction cards are two-sided. Each player secretly turns theirs to the faction they choose, with no talking first.
  3. Count the split without learning who is who: everyone drops a coin in a bag or cup, heads for Loyalist and tails for Reformist. Shake, pour and count.
  4. No faction may have more than half the players, rounded up. If one does, everyone chooses again. After three tries, set up by random draw from the bag.
  5. Hide your card under your two influence cards or in an opaque sleeve. To flip it, lift the cards, turn it over and put them back.

Action card

Take one action. With 10 or more coins you must Coup. Rows with an orange edge are new or changed.

Convert

Converting yourself costs 1 coin and converting another player costs 2, paid to the Treasury. The target flips their card and looks at it, and you don’t. Everyone sees that a conversion happened but not the result, so converting someone else is a blind gamble.

Attacking and misfires

Coup can target anyone and never misfires. Assassinate and Steal can target anyone too, but they can misfire.

  • Allegiance call: when targeted by Assassinate or Steal, before any challenge or block, you may call “Allegiance”. Both players show each other their faction cards, shielded from the table.
  • Same faction: the attack misfires. It is cancelled and the attacker’s coins are lost to the Treasury.
  • Different factions: false alarm. The caller pays 1 coin to the Treasury and the attack carries on.
  • No call: the attack goes ahead, even against an ally.
Assassinate, in order: declare and pay 3 coins. The target may call Allegiance. Anyone may challenge the Assassin. The target may block with the Contessa, which can be challenged. If nothing stops it, the target loses an influence.

Inquisitor and Contessa

  • Inquisitor: Swap and Examine are free. Checking a faction costs 1 coin to the Treasury.
  • Contessa: blocks the faction check (the Captain does not) as well as assassination. A block can be challenged: if the Contessa is real, the challenger loses an influence and the check is cancelled. If it was a bluff, the bluffer loses an influence and the check goes through.
  • The Inquisitor’s coin is spent even when the check is blocked.

How to win

  • Unity call: any player may call it between actions. Starting with the caller’s left, survivors reveal their faction cards one at a time. If all match the caller, the game ends and that faction wins. The reveal stops at the first mismatch and the caller loses one influence.
  • Last player standing also wins, as in normal Coup.
  • A player who is eliminated flips their faction card for everyone to see.

Optional: the Contessa can also block Convert another.
